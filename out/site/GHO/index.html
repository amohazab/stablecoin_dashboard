One counterfactual line asks what would happen to the money available to exit GHO if the GSM freezer had not stepped in during the modelled depeg. In the actual run, with the freezer acting, exit liquidity falls to just $220.4k. If the freezer instead does nothing and the GSMs stay open as exit routes, that figure jumps to $18.8M. The gap between these two numbers shows how much of GHO's exit capacity depends on those GSM venues staying open: freezing them removes almost all of the liquidity a holder could reach, while leaving them open restores it. The takeaway is that GHO's exit liquidity in stress is not a fixed pool sitting somewhere; it is largely conditional on whether the GSM freezer mechanism has been triggered.

GHO borrowers post collateral into Aave markets, and when a collateral price crash pushes a position's health factor below one, the whole position is liquidated across all its collateral and debt, not just the GHO slice; any shortfall is then attributed to GHO in proportion to its share of that borrower's debt. The GSMs, which mint and redeem GHO against approved stable assets, sit alongside this as a separate exit venue for holders rather than as part of the liquidation process itself.

Before the shock, collateral coverage across the book stands at 1.22, with 0.43 of positions already below full collateral backing. After the shock this coverage measure moves to 1.69, and the liquidation mechanism is credited with restoring 0.47 of coverage. Bad debt from the event comes to $8.6M, equal to 1.2% of supply.

That bad debt is not structurally zero: the relevant check found bad debt did occur under this grid, so the $8.6M figure stands as a real shortfall rather than a reprised or eliminated one.

The collateral that must be sold to cover the shortfall is $8.6M, measured against exit liquidity of $18.8M, giving a forced sales to exit liquidity ratio of 45.9%. This means the forced selling would take up nearly half of the available exit liquidity in this scenario, leaving a meaningful buffer unconsumed rather than exhausting it outright.

The admin-power table, row by row

Minting authority sits with a contract-automated holder (0x5300a1…192a), and the debt ceiling that limits how much can be minted is also controlled by a contract-automated holder (0x46aa10…5409). Both act on-chain without a waiting period before taking effect, and no address is recorded as able to veto either action. The oracle used for pricing collateral can be changed by a contract-automated holder (0x09e8e1…7dda) as well, again with no delay and no veto path shown.

Other levers that affect the system's safety are held in similar fashion. Parameters can be adjusted by a contract-automated holder (0x2ce01c…7285), which also controls freezing of a specific asset. Pausing exists in several forms: one pause function is controlled by a multisig (0x2cfe3e…aa30), while two other pause functions sit with contract-automated holders (0x6e5193…0047 and 0x733ab1…125b) covering separate scopes. None of these show a recorded delay or veto address.

There is no upgrade authority recorded for the core contract — its upgradeability is marked immutable — and no holder is recorded for blacklisting addresses or for seizing funds; both of those powers show no holder at all. So while backing itself cannot be upgraded away, the combination of automated, veto-free control over minting, the debt ceiling, and the oracle means the parameters that determine how much GHO can exist and how its collateral is priced can be changed immediately by the addresses named above, without any built-in delay for holders to react.

A notice fired because most of the exchange liquidity found for GHO sits in venues this report does not model. Off-venue liquidity discovered across pools comes to $29.0M, of which the off-venue curve mainnet liquidity, at $2.2M, is only a slice. In total, 92.4% of discovered DEX liquidity lies outside modeled venues. This means the exit-liquidity figures elsewhere in this report describe only a small modeled corner of where GHO actually trades, so real-world sale capacity could differ substantially from what those figures suggest.

A second notice fired because the frozen set of exit pools, locked in as of the freeze date of 2026-09-08, covers less of the discovered pool liquidity than the archetype's coverage target calls for. This is a dated waiver: the coverage shortfall was accepted rather than corrected, and it compounds the first point, since both stem from most liquidity for GHO sitting outside the venues this report tracks. Readers should treat any exit-liquidity metric for GHO as a lower bound drawn from a partial, frozen picture rather than a full market view.
