A collateral price crash triggers Aave liquidations for GHO, forcing the sale of collateral within the protocol's defined limits. Before the shock, collateral coverage stood at 1.20; after the shock, it rose to 1.76 as the mechanism restored 0.56 in coverage. The forced sales totalled $5.0M against exit liquidity of $62.0M, creating a ratio of 8.1%. Bad debt amounted to $5.0M, representing 0.7% of supply.

The binding side for liquidation capacity is the collateral that can be sold, which limits recovery minting rather than the ability to source GHO. Facilitator buckets hold up to $50.0M each, while GSMs retain $248.2M in mint headroom. No liquidity providers leave during this scenario.

The exit-liquidity ratio of 8.1% reflects forced sales divided by exit liquidity, not bad debt divided by exit liquidity. If the collateral sellable side were tighter, the binding constraint would shift to sourcing capacity.

A depeg of the paired stablecoin at its middle target triggers forced sales in the Member-2 headline scenario, where $62.9M of assets must be sold against exit liquidity of $735.2k. This results in a ratio of 8554.9% between the volume of forced sales and available exit liquidity.

The depth curve shows that at a price impact of 0.5%, the total pool depth is $61.9M, with a stability-module contribution of $61.2M. At 1.0% price impact, the total depth remains $61.9M while the stability-module contribution stays at $61.2M.

At 2.0% price impact, the total depth is $62.0M and the stability-module contribution is $61.2M. At 5.0% price impact, the total depth reaches $62.0M with a stability-module contribution of $61.2M.
