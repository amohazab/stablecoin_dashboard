For LUSD, the mechanism that matters in a collateral crash is the Stability Pool. When a trove's collateral ratio falls below the minimum, the Pool absorbs its debt directly, paying out collateral to Pool depositors; any shortfall the Pool cannot cover is redistributed pro rata onto surviving troves rather than sold into the market. Redemptions against troves are a separate channel, but on this scenario that path is excluded, so no redemption capacity is counted.

Before the shock, collateral coverage stands at 3.4984, and after the shock it is unchanged at 3.4984, meaning the liquidation mechanism restored no coverage gap because none opened. Bad debt is structurally zero on this grid: no trove reaches the minimum collateral ratio under the shocks modelled here. The first liquidation would only occur at an ETH shock of -81.63% (minimum ICR falling from 5.9890 to 1.1), Recovery Mode would only engage at -78.56% (system collateral ratio falling from 6.9968 to 1.5), and the system collateral ratio would only fall below the minimum collateral ratio at -84.28%. Since no forced sale occurs, the redistributed debt figure and the redistributed-positions-below-100 figure, which is how bad debt is defined for LUSD, are both $0, and the exit liquidity of $8.8M is untouched rather than exhausted in this scenario.

This test asks what happens if the paired stablecoin used in exit venues, referenced here as the Member-2 target, slips away from its own dollar peg. Because this token is structurally insulated, that kind of depeg cannot force a mechanical sale of the token itself: the assumptions describe it as structurally insulated, exposed through exit venues only. So there is no minting or redemption path that dumps supply just because the paired stablecoin wobbles.

That does not mean the token is unaffected. What changes instead is how much you could actually sell without moving the price much, because that liquidity sits in pools priced against the paired stablecoin. In the headline scenario, with the paired stablecoin at its middle target and no liquidity-provider flight, forced sales in the Member-2 headline depeg scenario come out at $0, exit liquidity in that same scenario is $8.2M, and the resulting ratio is 0. The zero forced-sales figure reflects the mechanism's design, not an absence of exposure.

The exit-liquidity curves show how the venue itself degrades as the paired stablecoin's price target worsens. At the mild target of 0.93, exit liquidity ranges from $6.9M at a 0.5% price impact up to $8.7M at 5.0%. At the deeper target of 0.88, the same curve runs from $6.5M to $8.2M, and at the milder target of 0.97 it runs from $7.2M up to $9.0M. As the paired stablecoin's target price falls, the depth available at any given price impact shrinks, meaning holders would face thinner exit liquidity and larger price impact for the same size of sale, even though no forced selling of the token itself is triggered by construction.
