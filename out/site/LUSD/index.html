A collateral price crash fits LUSD's liquidation mechanism because the Stability Pool absorbs debt from troves that fall below their maintenance requirement, and any remaining shortfall is redistributed pro rata to surviving troves rather than sold. This process means no forced sale exists on the absorption path, so the system does not rely on selling collateral against exit liquidity to cover losses.

The headline results show that collateral coverage remains at 3.25 before and after the shock, with zero bad debt and zero collateral that must be sold. The exit liquidity available in this scenario is $8.8M, and the ratio of forced sales to exit liquidity is zero because no sales occur.

LUSD that can be redeemed against troves before the redemption fee reaches its ceiling is not counted because the crash path is excluded from the calculation. The Stability Pool balance available to absorb liquidations in this scenario is $6.6M, which reflects the pool balance less the share of liquidity providers leaving.

A depeg of the paired stablecoin fits this token's mechanism because it is structurally insulated by construction, meaning exposure arises only through exit venues rather than forced sales. The recomputed exit-liquidity curves show that when the paired stablecoin trades at its middle target of $0.93, the available depth is $8.1M at a price impact of 1.0%. At a deeper stress of 5.0% price impact, the curve deepens to $9.3M, while a shallower move to 0.5% yields $7.3M in liquidity.

If the paired stablecoin trades lower at $0.88, the exit liquidity drops to $6.4M at 0.5% price impact and rises to $8.2M at 5.0%. Conversely, a higher trade price of $0.97 provides $7.1M at 0.5% and $9.0M at 5.0%, demonstrating how the mechanism-state quantities shift with the paired asset's level.
