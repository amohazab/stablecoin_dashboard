A price crash in ETH fits LUSD's liquidation mechanism because the Stability Pool absorbs debt from troves that fall below their maintenance collateral ratio, and any remaining shortfall is redistributed pro rata to surviving troves rather than sold. In this scenario, no forced sales occur against exit liquidity because the absorption path eliminates the need for a sell-side bound.

Collateral coverage remains at 3.72 before and after the shock, with zero bad debt generated. The system collateral ratio stays at 3.72, well above the Recovery Mode threshold, so no recovery mode is triggered.

The Stability Pool balance available to absorb liquidations in this scenario is $7.6M, which reflects the pool balance less the share of liquidity providers leaving. No LUSD can be redeemed against troves before the redemption fee reaches its ceiling because the crash path is excluded from the count.

A depeg of the paired stablecoin fits this token's mechanism because it is structurally insulated by construction, meaning exposure arises only through recomputed exit-liquidity curves rather than forced sales. When the paired asset trades at its middle target, the Member-2 headline scenario shows zero forced sales and a ratio of 0, indicating that no liquidations occur under these conditions.

Exit liquidity remains available across price impacts ranging from 0.5% to 5.0%, with totals varying between $7.0M and $8.9M depending on the specific depeg level. For instance, at a paired stablecoin price of 0.93 and a 2.0% price impact, exit liquidity reaches $7.7M, while deeper impacts like 5.0% push that figure to $8.6M.

These figures demonstrate that the system relies on existing pool depth to absorb shocks rather than triggering sell-side limits or bad debt events within the Member-2 headline framework.
