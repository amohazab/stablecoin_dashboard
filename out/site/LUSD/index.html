A price crash for LUSD triggers the Stability Pool and debt redistribution, not forced sales. Before the shock, collateral coverage sits at 3.33; after the shock it rises to 3.38 as the mechanism restores balance. The system records no bad debt because no trove reaches the minimum collateral ratio on the ruled grid, and the first liquidation would require an ETH shock of -30.26%.

The exit liquidity available in this scenario is $10.2M, yet forced sales equal zero since absorption offsets debt against the pool rather than selling collateral. Liquidity providers leave with a share of zero, leaving the full Stability Pool balance to absorb the impact.

This outcome holds across all shock levels shown: even when exit liquidity drops to $6.8M in later scenarios, the ratio of forced sales to exit liquidity remains zero because no forced sale exists on the absorption path.

A depeg of the paired stablecoin fits this token's mechanism because it is structurally insulated by construction, meaning exposure occurs only through exit venues rather than forced sales. The recomputed exit-liquidity curves show that when the paired stablecoin trades at its middle target of 0.93, the available depth is $9.5M at a price impact of 1.0%. At lower targets like 0.88, liquidity drops to $7.7M at the same impact level, while higher targets such as 0.97 provide $9.3M under identical conditions.

The stability-module contribution remains zero across all these scenarios, confirming that no forced sales occur in the Member-2 headline depeg scenario. Instead, the system relies entirely on the existing pool depth to absorb shocks without triggering liquidations. This design ensures that the token's value adjusts through market mechanics rather than internal sell-offs.
